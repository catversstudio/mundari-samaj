History and introduction

Mundari — 𞓞𞓐𞓪𞓐 𞓖𞓕𞓦𞓕𞓣 horo jagar, “the language of human beings” — is a North Munda (Kherwarian) language of the Austroasiatic family, spoken across the Chota Nagpur plateau and into Odisha, West Bengal, Assam, Bangladesh and Nepal. The word “Munda” itself means “village headman” in Mundari. This home page is only an introduction; every topic has its own page, linked below and in the menu above.

A short history

Ancient roots

The Munda languages belong to the Austroasiatic family — among the oldest language families of India — and Mundari has been spoken on the Chota Nagpur plateau for many centuries, long as an oral language of village, festival and song.

1899–1900

Birsa Munda's great uprising, the Ulgulan, made Mundari a language of resistance and identity; it led to the Chotanagpur Tenancy Act, 1908, protecting Mundari land.

1903

Hoffmann's Mundari Grammar was published, followed by the Encyclopaedia Mundarica (1924–1950) — the foundation works on the language.

1949

Schoolboy Rohidas Singh Nag of Mayurbhanj began creating the Mundari Bani script; its present 27-letter form was published in 1980 and revised in 2008.

2011

Mundari became an additional official language of Jharkhand. The Census of 2011 counted 16,34,150 speakers under the “Mundari” and “Munda” labels together.

2022

The Nag Mundari script entered Unicode 15.0 (block U+1E4D0–U+1E4FF), so Mundari Bani can be typed and read on phones and computers. Inclusion in the Eighth Schedule remains a living demand, not a granted status.

The language at a glance

Closest relatives: Santali and Ho. Four dialects — Hasada, Naguri, Tamaria and Kera. UNESCO lists Mundari as Vulnerable. Its word order is SOV, it counts in twenties, and it has two words for “we” — one including the listener, one excluding. All of this is explained, with the script shown beside every word, on the pages below.

The greeting is 𞓖𞓐𞓕𞓣 joar — hello, and also thank you.

How to use this site

Start with Script to learn the 27 letters, then Learn for flashcards and sentences, and keep the Dictionary open beside you. Every Mundari word on every page is shown in Mundari Bani script first, with its Roman form next to it, so you can read the script everywhere — in the pronouns, in the counting table, and in every other table.
